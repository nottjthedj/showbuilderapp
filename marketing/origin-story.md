# Four Keys — how the gangs formed, and why they're all here tonight

*The website story. In-world, narrated by TJ. Spoiler-safe: the vault reveal and the truth
about the man with no face stay in the room — nothing here contradicts them, and nothing
here gives them away.*

---

## The City

Every city tells a story about itself.

This one tells four, and they've never once agreed.

## The Vault

Downtown, under a building nobody photographs, there's a vault with eight figures inside it.

That isn't a rumour. Everybody knows it's there — the cops, the papers, the four crews, the
man who sold the concrete. It's been sitting there for fifty years, and in fifty years
nobody has opened it.

Not because they couldn't find it. Because of how it locks.

**It takes four keys.**

Four colours, four turns, and every one of them has to happen inside the same minute. The men
who put that money in didn't trust each other — not one of them, not for a second — so they
built a door that made sure none of them could ever take it out alone.

They thought they were being careful. What they actually did was leave their grandchildren a
problem.

Those men are dead now. Their crews are not.

## The Four

**The Corvettis** got here first. Off the boats, onto the docks, into the restaurants, and
eventually into everything — quietly, over three generations, one favour at a time. Don
Salvatore Corvetti runs them the way his father did: nothing written down, nothing said twice,
and a back room where the light is always warm. Their whole economy is respect. Money is just
how you keep score.

**Los Halcóns** came up through the marina in the eighties, and they did not come up quietly.
Rico Delgado believes the loudest crew wins, and for twenty years he has been proven right
often enough to never consider the alternative. Where the Corvettis hide, the Halcóns
*announce.* White suits, neon, a tiger in the house. If you have to ask whether it's theirs,
it isn't.

**The Street Kings** never wanted the city. That's what everybody else keeps getting wrong
about them. Marcus Wells wanted the block, and the block is what he has — every corner of it,
loyal, and nobody's had to be frightened into it. They're the only crew in this story that
people join because they want to.

**The Firm** bought their way in. No history, no neighbourhood, no bodies — just a glass tower,
a great deal of other people's money, and Preston Ashford Sterling III, who will tell you at
length that he has reinvented theft. They're the newest crew, the richest crew, and by a
considerable margin the least liked.

Four crews. Four keys. One each, handed down, never once all in the same place.

Until tonight.

## Why Tonight

The President is throwing a gala.

He's calling it a night of unity. A city standing together against crime. There'll be a
podium, a banner, a great deal of applause, and a man in a gold-accented navy suit telling a
room full of criminals how tough he is on criminals.

The gala is in the building. **The vault is in that building.**

Every crew got an invitation. And every crew knows the other three got one too.

So all four of them are going to walk through the same doors on the same night, in the same
clothes as everybody else, carrying the only four keys in the world that matter — and not one
of them is walking out with that money alone, because the door won't let them.

They need each other. They have never hated anything more.

## Why You

Here's the part the four of them don't advertise.

A key gets you a turn. It doesn't get you the room.

Four crews, one night, and not one of them has enough hands — not enough people to hold a
floor, run a job, move a package, watch a door, or crack a four-colour code against three
other crews doing exactly the same thing.

So tonight, all four are recruiting. In that building. Off that floor. From whoever shows up.

**That's you.**

You pick a colour at the door and you're theirs for the night. You'll play for them, score for
them, and if your crew opens that vault first, you were part of it.

Don't get wasted. Don't get busted.

## One Last Thing

Ask any of the four who really runs this city and watch what happens. They go quiet.

Then, if they like you, they'll tell you about a man in a black three-piece suit who has never
been photographed. Black gloves. Always turned away. The Corvettis are certain he takes a cut.
Rico swears he's been inside the house. The Firm has a file on him with nothing in it.

Four crews, four keys, and every one of them looking over their shoulder at a man none of them
can name.

I'd tell you what I know about him.

But you should probably hear that part in person.

**— TJ**
